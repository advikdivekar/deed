# Data card: DeedReader synthetic deeds

Generated by `data/gen.py` (seeded, reproducible: `python data/gen.py`). **Ground truth first, prose second**: a closed polygon with known calls is built, then rendered into deed language.

## What is in it
| split | n | styles | purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| train | 2,000 | modern_sym, modern_dash, modern_words, archaic_abbr, archaic_chains, ocr | SFT |
| val | 200 | same six | tune the confidence threshold only |
| test_id | 500 | same six, fresh polygons | in-distribution accuracy |
| test_heldout_style | 250 | `spelled` (every number in words) | never seen in training |
| test_heldout_unit | 250 | `varas` | a unit system never seen in training |

Each split uses an independent random stream and de-duplicates by text; `tests/test_gen.py` asserts no text appears in two splits and that held-out style/unit are absent from train.

## Geometry
3-12 sides (weighted to 4-7), star-shaped polygons (so never self-intersecting), 900-6,000 ft perimeter, either winding direction, random starting corner. Each call is re-aimed from the running *rounded* position, and the last call closes back to the point of beginning, as in real deeds. A parcel is kept only if it closes at 1:8,000 or better (the validator passes at 1:5,000). About 30% of modern-style deeds have 1-2 curves (chord bearing, chord length, radius, direction; arc length appears in prose as a distractor).

## Language variety
Bearings as `N 41°00'00" E`, `N41-00-00E`, "North 41 degrees 00 minutes 00 seconds East", `N. 41° E.`, "due north", spelled-out words. Units: feet, meters, chains (also "7 chains and 40 links"), poles/rods/perches, links, varas (held out). Mixed units inside one deed (8%). "to a white oak", "along to", "to the place of beginning", "more or less", acreage statements, and distractor clauses with numbers (easements, deed book/page, parcel numbers, "24 inch white oak"). The `ocr` style adds broken lines, hyphenation, `°`/`'` replaced by `*`/`` ` ``, and damaged letters.

## Output format
A compact line format, 3.8x fewer tokens than JSON (measured: 145 vs 547 completion tokens on average), converted to the JSON schema in code (`deedreader/fmt.py`). Monuments are canonical labels (`iron pin`, `stone`, `white oak`, ... `pob`).

## Limits (read these)
- Synthetic. The generator's grammar is far narrower than 200 years of real deeds. Real-world accuracy is **unknown** until we test on real descriptions; the in-distribution numbers say nothing about that.
- Held-out styles/units are held out *within my own generator*. They test generalization to new phrasing of the same underlying task, not to unseen legal conventions (e.g. "thence to the center of the creek and down its meanders").
- No deflection angles, azimuth-only bearings, station/offset, metes in "rods and perches" arithmetic, lot/block-only descriptions, or multi-parcel descriptions.
- The reference label set is a fixed 11-item ontology; real monuments are open-ended.
- Curves: chord form only. Non-tangent compound curves, spirals and arc-only descriptions are not generated.
- The validator's per-clause checks rely on calls being separated by `;` / `thence` / `then`. They fall back to whole-text checks if clause counts do not line up (this happens on ~0.3% of ground truth) and may behave differently on real deeds.
